Dear Church Family,

There is never a moment of our lives where worship is not happening. Whatever is at the center of the heart, mind, and habits—that is what we worship. As we abide in the Living Word, our mind’s attention and heart’s affection will be directed more and more towards Christ, making Him the worthy center of our worship. As the songbook of the Bible, the Psalms teach us how to worship in every season of life.

May we become a people who instinctually turn to the Word of God, seeking His face and voice. We pray that the Wednesday Psalter will be a means of returning to the simple practice of opening God’s Word and finding truth for our day.

God is still revealing Himself and His people are still responding. May we enter our gathering on Sunday already worshipping.

Psalm 3 (ESV)

Save Me, O My God

A Psalm of David, when he fled from Absalom his son.

[1] O Lord, how many are my foes!
   Many are rising against me;

[2] many are saying of my soul,
   “There is no salvation for him in God.” Selah

[3] But you, O Lord, are a shield about me,
   my glory, and the lifter of my head.

[4] I cried aloud to the Lord,
   and he answered me from his holy hill. Selah

[5] I lay down and slept;
   I woke again, for the Lord sustained me.

[6] I will not be afraid of many thousands of people
   who have set themselves against me all around.

[7] Arise, O Lord!
   Save me, O my God!
For you strike all my enemies on the cheek;
   you break the teeth of the wicked.

[8] Salvation belongs to the Lord;
   your blessing be on your people! Selah

REFLECT

Reflection

In Psalm 3, David declares his steadfast faith in the Lord amid violent opposition. The cornerstone of his faith is proclaimed in the center of the Psalm. In a time of Christianity that craves a dramatic, divine intervention ‘to strengthen one’s faith,’ Psalm 3 is a reminder that every breath, every heartbeat, every second of rest, is proof of the Lord’s faithfulness in our lives. May we see the Lord in the small moments of yesterday to strengthen our faith in the storms of today.
